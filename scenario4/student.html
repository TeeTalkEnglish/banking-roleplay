Day 4: Scam Safety

Learn how to spot scams, protect your bank details, and ask clear questions when something feels wrong.

Scenario 4A: Fake Bank Text

Customer Role: You received a text message that looks like it is from your bank.

Scenario: The message says your account is locked and asks for a one-time code or a link click.

Objective: Check whether the message is real, avoid the link, and ask how to report a phishing message.

Vocabulary: phishing, one-time code, link, scam, verification, account lock

Scenario 4B: Fake Safe Account Call

Customer Role: You answer a phone call from someone claiming to be the bank.

Scenario: The caller says your money is at risk and tells you to move it to a "safe account".

Objective: Recognise the scam, stop the call, and ask what a real bank would do instead.

Vocabulary: impersonation, scammer, urgent, transfer, safe account, callback

Scenario 4C: Unknown Card Payment

Customer Role: You are checking your bank app and notice a card payment you do not recognise.

Scenario: The payment appears to have happened after you used your card at a normal store.

Objective: Ask the bank to freeze the card, check the transaction, and explain how to dispute it.

Vocabulary: suspicious payment, dispute, freeze, merchant, refund, transaction

Scenario 4D: PayID Request

Customer Role: You want to buy something from a person online.

Scenario: The seller asks you to pay by PayID before you receive the item.

Objective: Decide if the request is safe, ask about buyer protection, and avoid sending money too quickly.

Vocabulary: PayID, seller, buyer protection, instant transfer, online marketplace, proof

Real-Life Banking Vocabulary

  • Phishing – a fake message or website that tries to steal your details
  • Impersonation – pretending to be your bank or another trusted organisation
  • One-time code – a temporary security code used to log in or verify you
  • Freeze – stop a card or account from being used for a short time
  • Dispute – ask the bank to investigate a payment
  • PayID – a fast way to send money using a phone number or email linked to a bank account
  • Callback – when you hang up and call the official number back yourself

Useful Questions at the Bank

  • Is this message real?
  • Should I click the link?
  • What should I do if someone asks for a code?
  • Can you check this payment with me?
  • How do I freeze my card?
  • Can I dispute this transaction?
  • How do I know if a PayID request is safe?
  • What is the safest thing to do next?

Australian Banking Notes

  • Banks will not ask for your full password or PIN in a text message or phone call.
  • If a message feels wrong, stop and contact the official bank number yourself.
  • Unknown card payments should be checked quickly.
  • Instant transfers can be hard to reverse, so always check before you send.
  • If you are unsure, ask the bank to explain the safest option first.

After the Roleplay — IELTS Speaking Practice

Part 1 — Introduction and Interview

(4–5 mins)

Answer template

Answer directly → explain → add an example.

Yes, I do. / No, not really. Usually, I ___ because ___. For example, one time ___. Overall, I think ___.

  • Have you ever received a suspicious message?

    Yes, I have. Once I received a message asking me to click a link and verify my account, but I ignored it and checked through the official app instead.

  • Do you usually check messages carefully?

    Yes. I try to read messages carefully because scams are becoming more common.

  • Do you prefer online banking or visiting a bank?

    I prefer online banking for convenience, but I visit a branch for important issues.

Part 2 — Cue Card

(1 min preparation · 1–2 mins speaking)

Describe a time when you had to protect yourself from a scam.

  • what happened
  • how you realised something was wrong
  • what action you took
  • and explain what you learned
Answer template

I'd like to talk about a time when ___. At first, I thought ___. However, I realised ___. To stay safe, I ___. As a result, ___. Looking back, I learned ___.

Sample answer

I'd like to talk about a time when I received a text message claiming to be from my bank. At first, I felt worried because the message said my account was locked. However, I noticed the link looked unusual. To stay safe, I ignored the message and contacted the bank directly. In the end, it turned out to be a scam. I learned that checking information first is very important.

Part 3 — Discussion

(4–5 mins)

Answer template

State opinion → explain → example → conclusion.

In my opinion, ___. One reason is ___. For example, ___. However, ___. Overall, ___.

  • Why do people fall for scams?

    People often fall for scams because scammers create urgency and emotional pressure.

  • Should banks do more to educate customers?

    Yes. Better education can help customers recognise scams earlier and make safer decisions.

  • Is online banking becoming too risky?

    Online banking has risks, but good security habits can reduce them significantly.

  • Do younger people handle digital safety better?

    Younger people may understand technology more easily, but everyone still needs awareness training.
