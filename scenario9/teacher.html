Teacher Guide

Scenario 8 - Buying Your First Home in Australia

Beginner to intermediate ESL lesson for a Vietnamese meat processing worker preparing to discuss home buying, mortgages and property in Australia.

Back to Scenarios Student Page

Teaching Objectives

  • Student can explain mortgage, home loan, deposit, interest rate and repayment in simple English.
  • Student can explain the difference between a home loan and a mortgage.
  • Student can explain the difference between an offer deposit and a down payment at settlement.
  • Student can describe the main timeline for buying a first home in Australia.
  • Student can compare property listings and home loan information using Australian websites.
  • Student can explain the different roles of property websites and mortgage brokers.
  • Student can ask useful questions to a mortgage broker, bank officer and real estate agent.
  • Student can calculate a deposit, loan amount and estimated monthly repayment.
  • Student can identify extra buying costs such as stamp duty, conveyancing, inspections, loan fees, moving costs and insurance.
  • Student can answer IELTS Speaking questions about housing and financial planning.

Key Concept Teaching Notes

Home Loan vs Mortgage

A home loan is the money borrowed from the bank. A mortgage is the legal agreement that secures the loan against the property if repayments stop.

Board flow: Save deposit - bank gives home loan - buy house - mortgage secures the loan - repay loan until fully paid.

Deposit vs Down Payment

A deposit is usually a smaller amount paid soon after an accepted offer and held in trust. A down payment is the larger savings contribution paid at settlement to reduce the loan amount.

Example: AUD 600,000 house, 20% down payment AUD 120,000, deposit already paid AUD 30,000, remaining settlement contribution AUD 90,000.

Buying Timeline

Teach the steps in order: save a deposit, check borrowing capacity, get pre-approval, search for a house, make an offer, pay deposit, final loan approval, settlement and move in.

Vocabulary Explanations

WordSimple ExplanationTeaching Tip
MortgageA loan agreement for buying property.Explain that the bank lends most of the money.
Home LoanMoney borrowed from a bank to buy a home.Students can use mortgage and home loan in similar contexts.
DepositMoney paid first by the buyer.Use 5%, 10% and 20% examples.
Interest RateThe cost of borrowing money, shown as a percentage.Connect rate changes to monthly repayment changes.
Fixed RateThe rate stays the same for a set period.Useful for predictable repayments.
Variable RateThe rate can change.Explain that repayments can rise or fall.
Borrowing CapacityThe amount the bank may lend.Income, expenses, dependants and debts matter.
Pre-approvalEarly bank approval before buying.Not a final guarantee.
Stamp DutyA property tax, depending on state rules.Mention that concessions may apply to first home buyers.
SettlementThe final transfer of ownership.This is when the buyer normally receives the keys.
Offset AccountA linked account that can reduce interest.Explain with salary sitting beside the loan.
RedrawA feature that may let borrowers access extra repayments.Contrast redraw with an offset account.
ConveyancingLegal work for transferring property ownership.Explain that buyers usually pay a solicitor or conveyancer.
Comparison RateA rate that includes interest plus some standard fees and charges.Teach students not to compare only the advertised interest rate.
PrincipalThe loan amount borrowed, before interest.Use "principal and interest repayments" as a collocation.
LenderThe bank or company that lends money.Ask students to name major Australian lenders.
Home EquityThe value of the home that the owner actually owns.Equity can grow when the loan decreases or the property value rises.

Useful for comparing homes and suburbs.Helps with paperwork and supports the buyer until settlement.

Why Brokers Can Help Migrants

Keep this practical and simple. Migrants may have temporary visas, overtime income or complex lending policies. A broker may help match the buyer with a suitable lender and reduce unnecessary credit checks.

Calculator Answer Guide

Using the default student-page example of a $520,000 house, $52,000 deposit, 6.25% interest and 30-year term:

  • 5% deposit: $26,000
  • 10% deposit: $52,000
  • 20% deposit: $104,000
  • Loan amount: $468,000
  • Estimated monthly repayment: about $2,882
  • Estimated total interest: about $569,360
  • Estimated total loan cost: about $1,037,360

Tell students this is an estimate only. Real repayments can include fees, insurance, offset features and rate changes.

Roleplay Teaching Tips

Mortgage Broker

Ask: What is your income? How much deposit do you have? Do you have debts? Encourage complete answers.

Bank Officer

Ask for payslips, ID and bank statements. Teach polite phrases like "Could you explain that again?"

Real Estate Agent

Practise property questions: price guide, inspection time, bedrooms, bathrooms, settlement and offers.

House Inspection

Prompt the student to ask about condition, repairs, transport, noise and why the owner is selling.

Required Focus Roleplay

The student is a meat processing worker who wants to buy her first house. The teacher is a mortgage broker.

Make sure the student asks these six questions:

  • How much can I borrow?
  • How much deposit do I need?
  • Which loan is better?
  • What is an offset account?
  • What is the comparison rate?
  • Should I choose fixed or variable?

IELTS Sample Answers

Part 1

Would you like to buy a home in the future? Yes, I would. I would like to buy a small home in Australia because renting can be expensive. I think owning a home would make me feel more secure.

Part 2

I'd like to buy a three-bedroom house in a regional city such as Wagga Wagga. It would be close to my workplace and not too expensive. To save for it, I would make a budget and put money into my savings account every payday. I think this home would give me stability in Australia.

Part 3

Should governments help first home buyers? Yes, I think governments should help first home buyers because buying a first home is very difficult. Support can help hardworking people enter the property market.
