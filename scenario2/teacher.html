Scenario 2 - Teacher

Lost Debit Card Teller Guide

Use this page to play the bank teller and prepare the learner for a real Australian banking problem: a missing card, possible fraud, identity checks, app safety, temporary money access and replacement-card delays.

Teacher setup

Student role: A customer in Australia whose debit card is missing.

Teacher role: A bank teller who asks verification questions, checks recent transactions and guides the customer through the safest next step.

Do not give the full solution immediately. Let the learner ask questions, then reveal realistic information based on the challenge you choose.

Realistic problem — Address mismatch The bank has the customer's old hostel address, but the replacement card needs to go to a new share house. Ask the customer for proof of their new address before ordering a replacement card. No change to transactions. "I can order the card, but your address is different on our system. Can you provide proof of your new address?"
Realistic problem — Rent due tomorrow The customer needs to pay rent before the replacement card arrives. Check whether online banking or an alternative payment method is available. No change to transactions. "You can still pay rent by bank transfer or PayID if online banking is working. Do you need help finding your BSB and account number?"
Realistic problem — Scam follow-up call Someone called claiming to be the bank and asked for the customer's one-time code. Warn the customer about scams and confirm the bank will never ask for full security codes. No change to transactions. "A real bank will not ask for your full password, PIN, or one-time security code. What should you do if you receive that call?"
Realistic problem — Card used on public transport The card was used for a tap-on transport payment after the customer lost it. Include the transport charge in the disputed transactions if the customer does not recognise it. Possible disputed payment: Metro Trains top-up or other transport charge. "Do you recognise this transport charge? If not, we can include it in the dispute."
Realistic problem — No passport today The customer only has a photo of their passport on their phone, not the physical document. Explain that a photo may not be enough for some services and ask for alternative ID or a return visit. No change to transactions. "A photo may not be enough for some services. Do you have another form of ID, or can you return with your passport?"
Realistic problem — Replacement fee question The customer is worried about extra charges for a replacement card. Tell them whether the first replacement fee is waived and confirm any costs before proceeding. No change to transactions. "Some banks waive the first replacement fee, but policies vary. It is always OK to ask about fees before agreeing."

Correction targets

  • Use "lost my debit card", not "lost my bank".
  • Use "block", "freeze" or "cancel" for the card action.
  • Use "transaction" for a payment shown on the account.
  • Use "I don't recognise this payment", not "I don't know this money".
  • Use polite direct questions: "Can you...?", "Could I...?", "How long...?"

Debrief answers

  • Freeze or block the card as soon as possible.
  • Check recent transactions and identify anything unknown.
  • Cancel the card if it might be stolen or compromised.
  • Ask the bank how to access money while waiting.
  • Never share PINs, passwords or one-time codes.
  • Keep address and phone details updated with the bank.
