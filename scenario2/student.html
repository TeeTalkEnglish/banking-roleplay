Scenario 2

Lost Debit Card

You are in Australia and you realise your debit card is missing. Speak to the bank teller, explain only what you know, and ask what to do next.

Bank vocabulary

Debit card A card that spends money from your everyday bank account. Block / freeze a card Stop the card so it cannot be used. Suspicious transaction A payment that might not be yours. Identity verification Questions or documents used to prove who you are. Replacement card A new card the bank sends after your card is lost, stolen or cancelled. Dispute a transaction Ask the bank to investigate a payment you did not make. Digital wallet A card saved on your phone, such as Apple Pay or Google Pay. Temporary card limit A short-term limit to reduce risk while the bank checks the problem.

Sample conversation

Customer: Good morning. I think I've lost my debit card.

Teller: I'm sorry to hear that. When did you last have it?

Customer: I used it yesterday afternoon at the supermarket.

Teller: Would you like me to block the card immediately?

Customer: Yes please. Can you also check if there have been any suspicious transactions?

Teller: Certainly. I'll need to verify your identity first.

Talk it through

After the conversation, answer these questions in your own words.

  • What should you do first if your debit card is lost in Australia?
  • Why is it safer to freeze or block a card quickly?
  • What details helped the teller understand your situation?
  • What should you check before you agree that a transaction is yours?
  • How can you pay for important things while waiting for a replacement card?
  • Why should you never share your PIN, password or one-time security code?
