Workplace Reality

You have now worked in Australia for two years as a meat processing worker.

You have saved money every payday. You no longer want to rent forever. You want to buy your first home.

Today you are meeting a mortgage broker. The broker will ask about your income, your deposit, your expenses and your borrowing capacity.

"Buying a home is a big step, but you can learn the language one question at a time."

Buying a House Timeline

Most first-home buyers move through these steps. The exact timing can change, but the order is a useful guide.

1. Save a Deposit

Build savings before speaking with lenders.

2. Check Borrowing Capacity

Estimate how much a bank may lend you.

3. Get Pre-Approval

Ask a bank or broker for early loan approval before making offers.

4. Search for a House

Use property websites and visit inspections.

5. Make an Offer

Tell the agent the price you are willing to pay.

6. Pay Deposit

Pay the agreed deposit after the seller accepts the offer.

7. Final Loan Approval

The lender checks the property and confirms the loan.

8. Settlement

Money is transferred and ownership changes to the buyer.

9. Move Into Your New Home

Collect the keys and move in after settlement.

Deposit vs Down Payment

These words can be confusing because people use them differently. In this lesson, use deposit for the smaller amount paid after an accepted offer, and down payment for the larger savings contribution used at settlement.

DepositDown Payment
Small amount paid after the seller accepts your offerLarger payment made on settlement day
Usually paid within 24-48 hoursComes from your own savings
Held in a trust or escrow accountReduces the amount borrowed from the bank
Shows the seller you are seriousHelps decide the final loan amount

Example

  • House Price: AUD 600,000
  • 20% Down Payment = AUD 120,000
  • Deposit already paid = AUD 30,000
  • Remaining due at settlement = AUD 90,000

While Watching Questions

Discuss your answer first. Click to reveal the suggested answer.

1. What is a mortgage?

A mortgage is a loan used to buy a property.

2. Why does a buyer need a deposit?

The deposit shows the buyer has saved money and reduces the amount they need to borrow.

3. What does a mortgage broker do?

A mortgage broker helps people compare loan options from different lenders.

4. What is borrowing capacity?

Borrowing capacity is the amount a bank may allow someone to borrow based on income, expenses and debts.

5. Why should buyers compare home loans?

Different banks may offer different interest rates, fees and loan features.

6. What should a first home buyer research?

They should research property prices, loan repayments, borrowing capacity and government support.

IELTS Speaking Part 1

Answer Template

I think __________ because __________. For example, __________. Overall, __________.

Question 1

Would you like to buy a home in the future?

Sample Answer

Yes, I would. I would like to buy a small home in Australia because renting can be expensive. I think owning a home would make me feel more secure.

Question 2

Is it difficult to save for a house?

Sample Answer

Yes, it is difficult because house prices are high and living costs are also expensive. However, a clear budget can help people save slowly.

Question 3

What kind of home would you like to buy?

Sample Answer

I would like to buy a small three-bedroom house. It does not need to be fancy, but it should be safe, clean and close to work.

IELTS Speaking Part 3

Discussion Template

In my opinion, __________. One reason is __________. For example, __________. However, __________. Overall, __________.

Question 1

Why are houses expensive in many countries?

Sample Answer

Houses are expensive because land is limited and many people want to live near jobs, schools and transport. Construction costs and interest rates can also affect prices.

Question 2

Should governments help first home buyers?

Sample Answer

Yes, I think governments should help first home buyers because buying a first home is very difficult. Support can help hardworking people enter the property market.

Question 3

Is it better to rent or buy?

Sample Answer

It depends on the person's situation. Renting is flexible, but buying can build long-term security. People should compare their income, savings and future plans before deciding.
