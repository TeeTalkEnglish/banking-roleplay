Day 3: International Money Transfers

Learn how to send money overseas, solve banking problems, and communicate effectively with Australian bank staff.

Scenario 3A: Family Transfer

Customer Role: You are an international student studying in Australia. Your mother in Vietnam needs financial assistance.

Scenario: You would like to send AUD $500 overseas for the first time. You are unsure how international transfers work.

Objective: Find out what information is required, how much the transfer costs, and how long the transfer will take.

Vocabulary: recipient, transfer fee, exchange rate, international transfer, processing time

Scenario 3B: Missing SWIFT Code

Customer Role: You need to send money overseas.

Scenario: You have the recipient's name and account number, but you do not have the SWIFT code.

Objective: Discover why the SWIFT code is important and learn what you need to do before the transfer can proceed.

Vocabulary: SWIFT code, bank details, verification, receiving bank, international banking

Scenario 3C: Wrong Account Number

Customer Role: You are sending money to a family member overseas.

Scenario: The bank discovers there may be a problem with the account information you provided.

Objective: Work with the teller to identify the mistake, correct the information, and complete the transfer safely.

Vocabulary: account number, beneficiary, bank records, confirmation, transfer request

Scenario 3D: Emergency Transfer

Customer Role: You need to send money overseas urgently.

Scenario: A family emergency has happened and your family needs money as soon as possible.

Objective: Ask about the fastest transfer options, possible fees, and realistic delivery times.

Vocabulary: urgent transfer, priority service, business days, additional fee, transfer tracking

Real-Life Banking Vocabulary

  • Recipient – person receiving the money
  • Beneficiary – official banking term for the recipient
  • SWIFT Code – international bank identification code
  • Transfer Fee – cost of sending money
  • Exchange Rate – value of one currency compared to another
  • Processing Time – how long the transfer takes
  • Verification – confirming information is correct
  • Transfer Limit – maximum amount allowed
  • Foreign Currency – money from another country
  • Transaction – movement of money

Useful Questions at the Bank

  • I'd like to send money overseas.
  • How much does the transfer cost?
  • How long will the transfer take?
  • What information do you need?
  • Do I need a SWIFT code?
  • Can I track the transfer?
  • Is there a transfer limit?
  • What happens if the details are incorrect?

Australian Banking Notes

  • Bring photo identification when visiting a bank.
  • Always double-check account numbers before sending money.
  • Large transfers may require additional verification.
  • International transfers often take 1–5 business days.
  • Different banks may charge different fees.

After the Roleplay — IELTS Speaking Practice

Part 1 — Introduction and Interview

(4–5 mins)

Answer template

Answer directly → explain → add an example.

Yes, I do. / No, not really. Usually, I prefer to ___ because ___. For example, when I needed to ___, I decided to ___. Overall, I think ___ because ___.

  • Have you ever sent money to someone?

    Yes, I have. I once sent money to my family overseas because they needed help. The process was simple after I prepared all the banking information.

  • Do you usually use cash or bank transfer?

    I usually prefer bank transfers because they are faster and easier to track.

  • Do you think international banking is difficult?

    At first it seems difficult because of the extra details required, but after learning the process it becomes manageable.

Part 2 — Cue Card

(1 min preparation · 1–2 mins speaking)

Describe a time when you had to solve a financial problem.

  • what happened
  • who was involved
  • what steps you took
  • and explain how you felt afterward
Answer template

I'd like to talk about a time when ___. This happened when ___. The situation became difficult because ___. To solve the problem, I first ___. After that, I ___. Eventually, ___. Looking back, I learned ___.

Sample answer

I'd like to talk about a time when I needed to send money overseas. My family needed financial support, so I visited the bank. First, I prepared the account number and identification. Then, I asked about fees and transfer times. In the end, the transfer was completed successfully. I felt relieved because everything arrived safely.

Part 3 — Discussion

(4–5 mins)

Answer template

State opinion → explain → compare → example.

"In my opinion, __. One reason is __. For example, __. However, __. Overall, ___. "

  • Should banks make international transfers easier?

    In my opinion, banks should simplify international transfers. One reason is that many customers feel confused by technical information and requirements. For example, first-time users may not understand terms such as SWIFT code or transfer fee. However, banks still need security procedures to protect customers. Overall, I believe the process should become easier while maintaining strong verification systems.

  • Why do people send money internationally?

    People often send money to support family members, pay tuition fees, or manage business activities.

  • Should banks make international transfers easier?

    Yes, because simpler systems reduce mistakes and help customers feel more confident.

  • Do you think digital banking will replace traditional banking?

    Digital banking is growing quickly, but many people still value face-to-face support for important transactions.
