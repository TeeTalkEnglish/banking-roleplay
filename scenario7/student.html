📚 Saving Money Vocabulary

These are common words Australians use when talking about money, budgeting and saving.

📋 Budget

/ˈbʌdʒɪt/

A plan showing how you will spend and save your money.

"I make a weekly budget."

monthly budget budget plan manage a budget

💰 Savings

/ˈseɪvɪŋz/

Money that you keep instead of spending.

"I put money into my savings every payday."

savings account build savings personal savings

💵 Income

/ˈɪnkʌm/

Money you receive from work or other sources.

"My income is paid every fortnight."

weekly income monthly income regular income

🧾 Expenses

/ɪkˈspensɪz/

Money you spend on bills and everyday living.

"My biggest expense is rent."

living expenses monthly expenses household expenses

🚑 Emergency Fund

/ɪˈmɜːdʒənsi fʌnd/

Money saved for unexpected situations.

"I keep an emergency fund."

build an emergency fund emergency savings

📈 Interest

/ˈɪntrəst/

Extra money paid by a bank on your savings.

"My savings earn interest."

interest rate earn interest

🥖 Needs

/niːdz/

Things you must buy to live.

"Food is a need."

basic needs essential needs

🛍 Wants

/wɒnts/

Things you would like but do not really need.

"Coffee every day is a want."

wants vs needs personal wants

🎯 Savings Goal

/ˈseɪvɪŋz ɡəʊl/

An amount of money you want to save.

"My savings goal is $5,000."

reach a goal set a goal

🔄 Automatic Transfer

/ˌɔːtəˈmætɪk ˈtrænsfɜː/

Money automatically moved into another account.

"I set up an automatic transfer every payday."

automatic payment automatic transfer

🏦 Balance

/ˈbæləns/

The amount of money in your bank account.

"My balance is growing."

account balance check balance

💳 Deposit

/dɪˈpɒzɪt/

Money paid into a bank account.

"My salary was deposited today."

bank deposit cash deposit direct deposit

💰 Three Ways to Save Money in Australia

After receiving your salary, there are several ways Australians save money. Let's explore three of the most common methods.

🔁

1. Automatic Transfer

The bank automatically transfers a fixed amount of money from your everyday account into your savings account every payday.

📖 Example

Your salary is A$1,200 every fortnight. You decide to save 20%. The bank automatically transfers A$240 into your savings account every payday.

💡 Why is it useful?
  • Builds a good saving habit.
  • You don't forget to save.
  • Easy to manage your money.
  • This method is often called "Pay Yourself First."
🤖

2. Smart Savings

Some banking apps can automatically move small amounts of spare money into your savings account.

📖 Example

After paying your rent and bills, your bank notices you have extra money. The app automatically moves A$50 into your savings account.

💡 Why is it useful?
  • No need to calculate every transfer.
  • Small amounts add up over time.
  • Helps people who forget to save.
🏦

3. High Interest Savings Account

Keep your money in a savings account that pays interest. The longer your money stays in the account, the more interest you can earn.

📖 Example

Commonwealth Bank's GoalSaver pays interest every month if you meet the account conditions.

💡 Why is it useful?
  • Your savings continue to grow.
  • You earn interest from the bank.
  • Great for long-term savings goals.
