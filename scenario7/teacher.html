📖 Lesson Information

Scenario Scenario 7 – Saving Money in Australia
Level A2–B1 (Beginner–Intermediate)
Recommended Age 16+
Lesson Length 60–90 Minutes
Main Skill Speaking
Supporting Skills Listening, Reading, Vocabulary, Financial Literacy
Theme Saving Money • Budgeting • Australian Banking
Real-life Situation A new meat processing worker wants to save money after receiving their first Australian salary.

🎯 Lesson Objectives

By the end of today's lesson, students should be able to:

  • ✅ Understand common Australian banking and saving vocabulary.
  • ✅ Explain the difference between needs and wants.
  • ✅ Read and discuss information about Australian savings accounts.
  • ✅ Compare electricity, phone, internet and bank services using Australian comparison websites.
  • ✅ Use English to ask questions about savings accounts.
  • ✅ Participate confidently in four financial roleplays.
  • ✅ Complete IELTS Speaking Part 1, Part 2 and Part 3 questions about money.

💡 Teaching Notes

Today's Focus

Many migrant workers receive their first Australian salary without knowing how to save effectively. Encourage students to think beyond vocabulary and discuss real financial decisions.

  • Ask students how they currently save money.
  • Encourage complete sentences.
  • Correct pronunciation after communication.
  • Ask follow-up questions naturally.
  • Use Australian examples whenever possible.

📚 Vocabulary Answer Key

Ensure students understand both the meaning and how the word is used naturally in Australia. Encourage students to make their own example sentence after each word.

Vocabulary Expected Answer Teaching Tip
Budget A plan for spending and saving money. Ask students if they have ever made one.
Savings Money kept for future use instead of spending. Discuss why people save money.
Income Money earned from work. Explain salary, wages and income.
Expenses Money spent on everyday living. Ask students to list their monthly expenses.
Emergency Fund Money saved for unexpected situations. Ask what emergencies might happen in Australia.
Interest Extra money earned from keeping money in a savings account. Explain "5% p.a." simply.
Needs Things people must buy. Food, rent and transport are good examples.
Wants Things people would like but don't really need. Coffee, games and luxury items are examples.
Savings Goal An amount of money someone plans to save. Ask each student for one saving goal.
Automatic Transfer Money automatically moved into another account. Explain "Pay yourself first".
Balance The amount of money currently in a bank account. Students often confuse balance with payment.
Deposit Money paid into a bank account. Different from withdrawing money.

🎥 Video Teacher Notes

Learning Outcome

Students should understand that saving money is a habit rather than simply earning more money. The video encourages students to:

  • Make a budget.
  • Separate needs from wants.
  • Save automatically.
  • Review monthly expenses.
  • Compare service providers before buying.

👀 While Watching — Suggested Answers

Question 1

What is a budget?

Suggested Answer

A budget is a plan showing how you will spend and save your money.

Question 2

Why should people save first?

Suggested Answer

Saving first helps people reach their financial goals and prepare for emergencies.

Question 3

What is the difference between needs and wants?

Suggested Answer

Needs are essential. Wants are optional.

Question 4

Why should people compare bills?

Suggested Answer

Different companies charge different prices. Comparing providers helps save money.

Question 5

What is an automatic transfer?

Suggested Answer

Money is automatically transferred into a savings account on payday.

Question 6

List three new words students heard.

Possible answers:

  • Budget
  • Savings
  • Interest
  • Emergency Fund
  • Expenses
  • Income

📝 Expected Student Responses

Question Example Response
Why should people save money? To prepare for emergencies and future goals.
How much should people save? About 10–20% of every salary.
Why compare electricity companies? Different providers charge different prices.
Why compare savings accounts? Some banks offer higher interest rates or better conditions.
What is one way to save money automatically? Set up an automatic transfer every payday.

🔍 Teacher Guide — Research Challenge

Students will use real Australian websites to compare products and services. There is no single correct answer. Focus on their reasoning and ability to explain their choice in English.

Research Task Teacher Notes Good Follow-up Questions
⚡ Electricity Students should use Energy Made Easy. Encourage them to compare supply charges and usage rates rather than choosing randomly. Why did you choose that company?
Was it the cheapest?
Would you recommend it?
📱 Mobile Plan Students should compare at least three providers. Look for price, data allowance and contract length. How much data do you need each month?
Do you need international calls?
🌐 Internet Students should compare price, download speed and contract period. Which internet speed would you choose?
Why?
🏦 Savings Account Students should compare interest rate, fees and bonus conditions. Which account earns the highest interest?
Would you choose it?

🎭 Teacher Roleplay Guide

🏦 Roleplay 1 — Bank Employee

You work for Commonwealth Bank. The student wants to open a savings account.

Ask:
  • Why do you want to save money?
  • How much do you want to save every month?
  • Would you like automatic transfers?
  • Do you understand bonus interest?
Encourage students to ask:
  • What interest rate do you offer?
  • Does this account have monthly fees?
  • Can I transfer money automatically?

💰 Roleplay 2 — Friend Helps Budget

Your friend has just started working in Australia. Help them make a weekly budget.

Ask:
  • How much do you earn each week?
  • How much is your rent?
  • How much do you spend on food?
  • How much should you save?

🛒 Roleplay 3 — Financial Coach

The student spends too much money every payday. Help them improve their spending habits.

Suggested advice:
  • Cook meals at home.
  • Take lunch to work.
  • Stop buying unnecessary coffee.
  • Compare electricity providers.
  • Compare phone plans.
  • Save before spending.

🎯 Roleplay 4 — Savings Goal

Help the student create a savings plan.

Ask:
  • What are you saving for?
  • How much money do you need?
  • How long will it take?
  • How much can you save every payday?
Extension Questions
  • What happens if an emergency occurs?
  • Will you still reach your goal?
  • How can you save faster?

⚠ Common Student Mistakes

Incorrect Correct
I very like save money. I really like saving money.
I open saving account. I want to open a savings account.
I save for emergency. I save money for emergencies.
Bank give me interest. The bank pays me interest.
I transfer automatic. I set up an automatic transfer.

🚀 Fast Finish Activities

  • Rent
  • Food
  • Transport
  • Phone
  • Savings
  • Entertainment

🏦 Compare Banks

Ask the student:

  • Which bank did you choose?
  • Why?
  • What is the interest rate?
  • Would you recommend it?

🛒 Needs vs Wants

Give the student an item. Ask:

  • Need or Want?
  • Why?

Examples:

  • Coffee ☕
  • Netflix 📺
  • Rent 🏠
  • New Shoes 👟
  • Holiday ✈️

💬 Free Conversation

Continue speaking naturally for five minutes. Suggested questions:

  • What would you do if you won $10,000?
  • How much should people save?
  • Should young people invest?
  • Is budgeting difficult?

🏠 Homework

Research Task

Students should complete the following before the next lesson.

  1. Compare two Australian savings accounts again using the Canstar website.
  2. Write five new banking vocabulary words.
  3. Write a simple weekly budget using your own income.
  4. Prepare a two-minute speech answering: "How do you plan to save money in Australia?"
  5. Be ready to discuss your answers in the next lesson.
